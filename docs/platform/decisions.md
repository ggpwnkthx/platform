# The decisions of the platform

The decisions of the **contracts** are in the documentation of their
workspace:

| Workspace | The decisions |
| --- | --- |
| `packages/gateway/` | [docs/decisions.md](../gateway/decisions.md) |
| `packages/access/` | [docs/spec.md](../access/spec.md) — the decisions are in the specification |
| `packages/data/` | [docs/decisions.md](../data/decisions.md) |

This document holds the decisions of the **shape of the repository**:
the ones that the merge of the three repositories took.

| # | The decision | The evidence | The status |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | The three services are three workspaces of one repository, and not one service. | The three are separate deployments with separate secrets, separate databases and separate contracts. A shared source would make a change of one of them a change of the build of the others, and it would break the deployments that run one of them alone. The boundary between them is the boundary of their specifications, and the merge keeps it. | resolved |
| 2 | The workspace never imports the source of another workspace. | The services talk over HTTP, and the contracts of the boundaries are the ones of the specifications. The check of the architecture of each workspace refuses an import of another workspace, and the compatibility suite starts the three services and performs the acceptance scenario through them. | resolved |
| 3 | The build context of the three images is the root of the repository. | The dependencies of a workspace are installed by the root `bun install`, and the lockfile of the root holds the three of them. An image that is built from the directory of a workspace installs a tree that the other workspaces do not share, and the lockfile of the root would refuse it. | resolved |
| 4 | There is one version, and it is the version of the platform. | The three services were three repositories, and each of them carried a version of its own; the repositories are retired, and the lines they carried are not continued here. One version for the repository, of the tag, of the three images, of the three binaries **and** of the status of the three specifications: the status of a specification is the release that carries it, and a specification that names another release describes a behaviour the code does not have. The check of the versions proves that they agree. | resolved |
| 5 | The images and the environment of the services are renamed. | The name of an image and the prefix of the environment are coordinates of a deployment: a deployment that pinned the name of an image, or that set a variable of the environment, has to name the new ones — that is a migration, and it is written down in the changelog. The prefixes the services carried were built on the names of the repositories they were published from, and those repositories do not exist any more: a variable of a retired name is a variable that no service reads, and a deployment that sets it believes it configured something. | resolved |
| 6 | The published client of the data service keeps its name. | `@data-backend/client` is a package that consumers install. Renaming it breaks them, and the merge of the source is not a reason to break a consumer. | resolved |
| 7 | The baseline of the compatibility is the revision of the repository. | The references of the suite were the gateway and the access service in two other repositories, at two commits. They are workspaces of this repository now, and a suite that clones a copy of the code of the repository proves less than a suite that runs the code of the revision. The overrides of the environment still name a published revision, and a run with an override is a run of a candidate. | resolved |
| 8 | There is one tagger, one workflow of the continuous integration, one workflow of the images and one workflow of the binaries. | There were three of each, they were close to each other and they drifted. The jobs of the three services are the jobs that existed, and they are the same jobs: a gate that exists for one service and not for another is a gate that was forgotten. | resolved |
| 9 | The documentation of the platform is one tree, and it is at the root of the repository. | A contract is normative, and it is read next to the documentation that is common to the three services. The two used to live apart — the documentation of the platform at the root of the repository, the documentation of a contract in its workspace — and a reader of one had to know where the other was. They are one tree now: `docs/platform/` for the parts that are common to the three, `docs/gateway/`, `docs/access/` and `docs/data/` for the contracts. The boundary of the **code** did not move: a workspace never imports the source of another one. | resolved |
| 10 | The tools of the platform are declared at the root, and the dependencies of a service are declared in its workspace. | The compiler, the linter, the formatter and the checker of the dead code are the tools of the three of them, and they are one version for the three. A dependency of a service is declared by the service that uses it, and a workspace that does not use it does not carry it. | resolved |
| 11 | The specifications of the workspaces name their services by their role, and so does the platform. | The specification of a service is the contract of that service, and a contract that names a service by a name that names nothing is a contract that reads badly: the name of a retired repository is the name of nothing, and the reader of the contract has to be able to name the service it is about. The platform, the workspaces, the images, the environment and the documentation name the three by their role — the gateway, the access service and the data service — because the role is what the repository is about. | resolved |
| 12 | The retired repositories are not named by the platform, and the check says so. | The three repositories of the services are gone: a clone of them fails, a release published in them is not reachable, and a document that tells an operator to clone one sends it to a dead end. The platform names one repository, one set of images and one release line, and the check of the repositories fails the build when a file names a repository that the platform does not have. The check does not name the repositories that are gone: it refuses every name but the ones of the platform, because a list of dead ends is a list that names them again. The record of what was is not kept either: the plan of the merge and the histories of the three services are gone, and the version lines the retired repositories carried are not continued — the platform has one release line. | resolved |
| 13 | The services are named by their role, and their vocabulary is named by their role too. | The three services were published from three repositories of their own, and the names they carried are the names of those repositories. The repositories are retired, and a name that names a repository that does not exist names nothing: the platform names the three by their role — the gateway, the access service and the data service — in the workspaces, in the code, in the deployments and in the documentation. The vocabulary of a service moves with it: the metrics, the headers of the identity, the cookies, the tables, the namespaces of the shared state and the prefixes of the environment carry the name of the role of the service. A dashboard that watches a metric of a retired name watches a metric that is never published, and a deployment that sets a variable of a retired name sets a variable that no service reads. | resolved |
| 14 | The check of the vocabulary refuses the identifiers that do not carry the name of the role of their service. | The services are named by their role, and the identifiers of a service — the prefixes of its environment, the names of its metrics and the name it answers with — carry the name of that role. An identifier that carries the name of a retired repository is an identifier that nothing reads: a deployment that sets it sets a variable that no service reads, and a dashboard that watches it watches a metric that is never published. The check does not name the names that are gone: it refuses every identifier but the ones of the roles, because a list of dead ends is a list that has to be kept — and one that names them again (decision 12). | resolved |
